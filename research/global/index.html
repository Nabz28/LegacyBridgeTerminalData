The call

The world is at high levels and decelerating, and the desk board is positioned for the levels. Score the same 23 desks on direction of travel instead of level and 17 of them flip stance. China goes overweight to underweight. Base metals, agriculture and automation do the same. That is not a data problem to be fixed before acting; it is the finding.

Underneath that, three things are true at once and cannot all stay true.

The S&P sits at the 99th percentile of its own year, while 48% of the 252 things we price are more than 20% below their own highs.

Credit spreads are as tight as they have been in eight years, while the real 10-year yield is near its high.

And the four Asian technology overweights that dominate the board all rest on the same TSMC revenue print from 1 June.

Five things that matter

  1. The real long rate is the regime, and it is not in the regime tag. Real 10y at 1.8242% (4 Aug) is up 22.2% over 63 periods and 38.7% over 252 while the Fed funds rate fell 16.2% over the same year. The 2-year at 4.20% now sits 57bp above the policy rate. The market is not asking for more easing; it is undoing the easing already delivered. Four independent desks name this one series as their flip trigger. It is 28bp from the level at which the AI, semiconductor and software overweights mechanically break.
  2. Seventeen of twenty-three stances are an artefact of scoring levels. The dial blends driver level z-scores with no rate-of-change term, so a driver at a five-year high that has started rolling over scores identically to one still rising. Rescored on 63-period momentum with identical weights and directions, China-HK-Taiwan moves from +1.15 to −0.70 and base metals, agriculture and automation all cross from overweight to underweight.
  3. Twenty-three desk labels contain three independent bets. Under a Marchenko-Pastur noise floor only two eigenvalues clear at 60 days and three at 252. The first factor is not market beta: software loads 0.042 and platforms 0.087, while metals, grid, automation, semiconductors and North Asian hardware load 0.28 to 0.30. It is the industrial capex and electrification build. Semiconductors, Japan/Korea and base metals are all overweight, all top loaders, and correlate 0.80 to 0.89 with each other. The board is a levered long on one factor held through six labels.
  4. Five conviction-4 calls do not survive their own data. Dropping drivers whose latest print is more than 35 days old and renormalising, semiconductors falls from 1.10 to 0.31, AI-compute from 1.07 to 0.43, automation from 0.52 to −0.14 and aerospace from 0.63 to 0.09. The Asia technology cluster leans on one 67-day-old TSMC number expressed four times.
  5. Indonesia is unhedgeable and that is useful. Its r² against seven global macro factors is 0.052, the lowest of 23 desks against a median of 0.384. Ninety-five percent of the variance is idiosyncratic, which means no global instrument offsets it and no global model prices it. It is the one place where local knowledge is the entire edge, and it is where the book's losses are.

Three disagreements inside the data

  • Index versus constituent. Euro Stoxx, DAX and European broad equity all sit at the 100th percentile of their trailing year. The median instrument across all 23 baskets is 10 to 34% below its own high, and only 19.4% of names are within 5% of theirs. Over 63 days just 46% of names are positive and the top decile took 53.7% of all gains.
  • Credit versus rates. Credit conditions at the 99.6th percentile and VIX at the 13th say no stress. Real rates at the 96.8th, housing momentum at the 11th and cyclical appetite at the 36th say the rate-sensitive economy has already been priced. Bond volatility rose 7.6% over 63 days while equity volatility fell 10.5%.
  • Drivers versus tape. Chemicals is overweight at 1.07 while its basket is 14.4pp behind its benchmark over 63 days. Consumer brands is underweight while its basket outperformed XLP over 63 days. Software was cut to Neutral in the same run its basket printed +8.7% in five sessions and holds the four best 63-day performers in the entire universe.

Global regime

The engine tags the world expanding growth, sticky inflation, liquidity easing, risk-on with full data coverage. Growth is the only leg that survives inspection unchanged.

Growth: confirmed. Payrolls at 158,984k (1 Jun) added 57k on the month and 392k over five. Unemployment is 4.2%, down from 4.5% in November. Manufacturing weekly hours at 41.6 sit at the 100th percentile of 58 months, and because hours are a bounded series that percentile carries real information in a way a price index percentile does not.

Inflation: mis-specified. The engine fires on core CPI at the 98th percentile. That is an index level at rank 56 of 57 months on a series that only rises, and it carries close to zero information. The actual rates of change tell a different and more useful story: core CPI −0.02% on the month and +0.98% over five (roughly 2.4% annualised), headline +1.83% over five (4.5%), and PPI final demand +3.22% over five (7.9%). The pipeline is running about three times core. That is not a policy problem, it is a margin problem, and it argues for pricing power over volume.

Liquidity: easing in quantity, tightening in price. M2 at 23,155.2 (30 Jun) is up 3.28% in five months, genuine acceleration. But the Fed funds rate is flat over 63 periods after its year of cuts, the ECB deposit rate actually rose from 2.00 to 2.25 in mid-June, and the real 10-year is up 22.2% over the same 63 periods. The engine's three liquidity votes captured the quantity of money and missed the price of it. The price side is what binds.

Risk: narrow. Risk-on is derived from VIX at the 13th percentile and credit at the 99.6th. Both are the most reflexive, least forward-looking inputs available. Set against cyclical appetite at the 36th percentile and housing momentum at the 11th, the honest reading is that the marginal rate-sensitive dollar has already left the building while the index has not noticed.

The retag. Expanding growth, pipeline inflation rather than core, liquidity easing in quantity and tightening in price with the price side binding, and narrow risk-on. Full data coverage gave the original tag false authority: complete data is not correct weighting.

Rates: the binding constraint

The whole curve moved against the policy rate this year. Fed funds fell 16.2% over 252 periods to 3.63%. Over the same window the 2-year rose 6.6% to 4.20%, the 10-year 6.0% to 4.63% and the 30-year 5.9% to 5.18%. The 30-year sits at the 98.4th percentile of its trailing year, the 10-year at the 96.4th, the 2-year at the 94.0th.

The 2s10s curve at 0.43 steepened 22.9% over the last 5 and 21 periods but still sits at only the 16.7th percentile of its year, and the steepening came from the long end: the 30-year rose 3.81% over 21 periods against the 2-year's 1.69%. That is a bear steepener. It is the shape that appears when the market reprices term premium or the neutral rate, not when it front-runs cuts.

Decomposing the 10-year gives a breakeven of 2.81% against a real yield of 1.8242%. Real rates rose 22.2% over 63 periods while the nominal rose 4.0%. Almost the entire long-end move is real. If that is term premium rather than inflation expectations, further policy cuts steepen the curve and raise the discount rate on exactly the long-duration assets the board is overweight. Long duration is not the hedge in this configuration.

Practical consequence: set the house tripwire on real 10y rather than on Fed funds. 2.10% is the level at which the AI-compute and semiconductor overweights break on their own stated flip conditions. 1.65% is the level at which the underweights in consumer brands and autos become hard to defend. At 1.8242% we sit 28bp from the first and 17bp from the second.

The five country desks

United States · Neutral, score +0.40

The only desk where macro factors explain nearly all the movement (r² 0.946) and the only one whose stance improves when stale drivers are dropped, from 0.43 to 0.92. Its basket is 0.9% off its high, the tightest of any desk. The credit-conditions relationship that historically drove it has broken: 252-day correlation +0.41 has collapsed to +0.01 over 60 days. US equities have stopped responding to the credit signal that is sitting at its 99.6th percentile.

China, Hong Kong & Taiwan · Overweight conviction 4, score +1.24

The highest score on the board and the least supported by its own equities. 81.8% of the score comes from a currency (USD/CNY at the 0th percentile, its strongest of the sample), a commodity (copper at the 100th) and a TSMC revenue print 67 days old. The two equity drivers contribute 0.926 of 5.102 gross. MCHI is 16.6% below its high and −4.9% over 63 days; CSI 300 is −3.3%. Rescored on momentum the desk goes to −0.70. This is the single most fragile overweight on the board.

Japan & Korea · Overweight conviction 4, score +1.16

Carries the highest risk-off sensitivity of any desk at −15.7% on the combined shock, with a dollar beta of −0.66 and an SPX beta of 1.40. The yen at 158.30 weakened 2.9 sigma in five sessions. KOSPI is the extreme case of the whole report's theme: +16.5% over five sessions after −18.1% over 21, and still 27.6% below its 52-week high. Nikkei +7.9% over five after −4.9% over 21. Violent two-way movement inside a market up 84% on the year at the median.

Eurozone · Neutral, score +0.47

The Neutral is driver cancellation, not a mixed tape. Three overlapping European equity price series contribute +4.75 while the two genuinely exogenous inputs, the ECB deposit rate and TTF gas at +61.8% over 252 periods, pull the other way. Euro Stoxx, DAX and VGK are all at the 100th percentile of their year and the basket is at its high. Note the ECB hiked in June while the tag still reads easing.

Indonesia · Underweight conviction 3, score −0.79

Covered in full below. In short: 88% of the underweight is two z-scores on trending price levels, which restate the drawdown and the depreciation rather than adding information, and the stance sits within 2.5% of its own stated invalidation on both legs while the index has risen 8.0% in 21 sessions.

Market sentiment

Risk appetite is real at the index level and absent underneath it. The S&P at 7,719 is at the 99th percentile of its own year and +22.6% over 252 periods; the Nasdaq 100 at the 90th and +27.9%; Euro Stoxx, DAX and European broad equity all at the 100th. Bitcoin, the purest available sentiment instrument, is at the 20th percentile and −29.5% over the year. Those cannot both be risk appetite.

The last five sessions were violently positive in Asia: KOSPI +16.5%, Taiwan +11.4%, Nikkei +7.9%, semiconductors +7.9%. Over 21 periods the same markets are −18.1%, −4.2%, −4.9% and −3.0%. This is a sharp drawdown followed by a sharper bounce, not a trend.

News flow across 271 items is quiet and slightly positive: 188 neutral, 59 bullish, 24 bearish. Attention concentrates on Indonesia (29 stories, tone +0.21) and AI-compute (20, tone +0.05), where the dominant thread is Asian technology weakness led by an SK Hynix fall, set against an Alibaba model release. Internals: what the indices hide

Two-thirds of instruments are above their 50-day average and only 54.8% above their 200-day. Only 19.4% sit within 5% of their own 52-week high while 48.0% are more than 20% below it. Over the last month 184 names rose and 68 fell, with a median gain of 5.49%. Over three months only 46% are positive, and the top tenth captured 53.7% of all the gains.

That combination has one reading. A broad, recent, powerful bounce is running inside a market whose average constituent spent the preceding quarter falling and remains well below its highs. The indices are at records because the narrow group that never fell is carrying them.

Cyclicals lead the bounce, up 8.6% against 3.7% for the secular names. But cyclicals have the weaker long-term participation: 49.5% above their 200-day average against 59.7%. Rotation off depressed levels, not trend continuation.

Cross-sectional dispersion of 9.83% over 21 periods is the number that matters for a stock picker. Selection is being paid right now, and it is being paid most in the desks with the highest idiosyncratic share.

Factor structure: three bets, not twenty-three

A 23 by 23 correlation matrix estimated on 55 observations cannot be read at face value. Under a Marchenko-Pastur null with q = 0.418, the largest pure-noise eigenvalue accounts for 11.79% of variance. At 60 days the first factor clears at 35.0% and the second at 15.70%; the third at 11.31% does not. At 252 days three clear. The honest count is three independent bets, six at the very outside, across twenty-three labels.

The first factor is not market beta. Software loads 0.042 and platforms 0.087, which high-beta growth would never do. What loads is base metals 0.304, the US 0.302, grid infrastructure 0.299, automation 0.286, semiconductors 0.282 and Japan/Korea 0.278. Five of those six persist in the 252-day estimate, so it is structural rather than a 60-day artefact. It is the industrial capex and electrification build, corroborated on the driver side by copper at the 100th percentile, US manufacturing hours at a five-year high and TSMC revenue at its own maximum.

Average pair correlation reads 0.2598 at 252 days and 0.2284 at 60, which looks like healthy divergence. It is not. Correcting the 60-day eigenvalues for small-sample inflation gives a true leading eigenvalue of 32.87% against 33.79% on the long window, and the top two factors account for 45.8% now versus 45.2% before. Nothing has converged and nothing has diversified. The windows also share 55 of 230 observations, so they are not independent samples.

The consequences for risk budgeting are direct. Semiconductors, Japan/Korea and base metals are all overweight, all top loaders, and correlate 0.80 to 0.89 with one another. Treating them as three positions double-counts one factor roughly threefold. Precious metals, which a risk system will usually credit as a diversifier, carries correlation 0.785 to the universe and a beta of 2.21: it is a levered risk-on position, not a hedge. The only genuine offsets to the first factor available in this universe are oil-gas (beta −0.08), healthcare (0.21) and software (0.35), and the board is Neutral on all three.

Commodities

The complex is split, and the split is the information. Copper at 6.725 is at the 100th percentile of its trailing year and +9.6% over 63 periods. Brent at 82.56 is −18.5% over the same window and WTI −18.6%. Copper and crude do not usually disagree about global demand by 28 points over a quarter.

Two readings compete. Either copper is right and it is an electrification and grid story that crude does not participate in, which the first principal component supports. Or copper is a positioning artefact, which its 94th-percentile speculative long supports. The tiebreaker should be Chinese equities confirming the demand, and they are not: CSI 300 −3.3% and MCHI −4.9% over 63 periods.

Positioning and crowding

One market is genuinely crowded. Copper speculative positioning sits at the 94th percentile of its three-year range. The price is already at the 100th. This is the highest-conviction fade on the board, and the base metals desk is overweight into it with copper supplying 85% of that overweight's score.

The dollar is the second stretched market at the 87th percentile, consistent with the dollar index at the 81st. Everything else is unremarkable: gold 33rd, WTI 20th, silver 12th. The absence of crowding in gold and oil matters as much as the presence of it in copper, because it means the recent moves in both were not positioning-driven.

What the dial board misses

Twenty-three desk analysts were run independently over the same evidence packs with instructions to interrogate the machine stance rather than restate it. They converged, without coordination, on three structural criticisms. All three check out.

One: the score has no rate-of-change term

The dial blends level z-scores of each driver. A driver at a five-year high that has begun rolling over scores identically to one still rising. Rescoring all 23 desks with identical weights and directions but on the z-score of each driver's 63-period change, seventeen stances flip. China-HK-Taiwan moves +1.15 to −0.70, base metals +0.80 to −0.72, agriculture +0.65 to −0.85, automation +0.52 to −0.67. Only precious metals, Eurozone, transport, grid, healthcare and coal hold the same call on both methods.

This does not make the level score wrong. Level is the correct question for a driver where the level is the economics: a high oil price genuinely does help producers. It makes the board incomplete, and it means every overweight currently on it should be read as a statement about where things stand, not where they are heading.

Two: stale monthly drivers carry full weight

A driver last printed on 1 June is weighted exactly like one that traded this morning. Dropping every driver older than 35 days and renormalising, five stances do not survive: semiconductors 1.10 to 0.31, AI-compute 1.07 to 0.43, automation 0.52 to −0.14, aerospace 0.63 to 0.09, and the US desk strengthening 0.43 to 0.92. Transport-logistics carries the highest stale dependence at 54% of gross contribution.

The concentration is worse than the count suggests. Semiconductors, AI-compute, Japan/Korea and China all lean on the same TSMC revenue figure from 1 June. Four conviction-4 overweights, one number, 67 days old, and TSMC reports monthly.

Three: several dials are single-driver models wearing four drivers

Precious metals takes 69% of its score from real rates, base metals 85% from copper alone, transport 54% from manufacturing hours, Indonesia 54% from the rupiah, semiconductors 48% from TSMC revenue, aerospace 91% from the S&P level. In each case the other three drivers are decoration. A dial that is really a single-variable model should be labelled as one, because its failure mode is that variable being wrong rather than a diversified set of inputs all being wrong together.

These are criticisms of a system built yesterday, surfaced by that system's own reasoning layer on its first full run. They are being reported rather than quietly patched because the board is live and the stances are on it now. The fixes are known: add a momentum term, decay driver weight by staleness, and flag single-driver concentration on the dial itself.

Risks and what would change the view

The crowded trade

Copper at the 100th percentile with speculative length at the 94th, against Chinese equities that will not confirm the demand. An unwind would hit base metals, grid and automation simultaneously because all three load on the same factor. Confirmation: China activity data on 14 August failing to show industrial demand, or copper breaking with CSI 300 still falling.

The real-rate break

Real 10y at 1.8242% is 28bp below the level at which the AI, semiconductor and software overweights break on their own stated conditions. A move to 2.10% mechanically breaks four desks at once. Confirmation: the 30-year continuing to lead the selloff, since a bear steepener driven by term premium is not fixed by policy easing.

The stale-driver reversal

Four overweights depend on a TSMC revenue print from 1 June. TSMC reports monthly. The next print either confirms the position or removes 40 to 70% of the score behind four conviction-4 calls in a single day. Confirmation: the next TSMC monthly revenue release.

The concentration that does not show

The book's measured factor concentration of 24.8% is flattered by Indonesian idiosyncrasy. Half the book depends on Indonesian local conditions that no global factor prices and no global instrument hedges. Confirmation: the three Indonesian positions moving together on a domestic event, which is precisely what a correlation model built on global factors will not predict.

The narrow tape

Indices at the 99th to 100th percentile with 48% of names more than 20% below their own highs and the top decile taking 53.7% of all gains. If leadership breaks there is nothing underneath it. Confirmation: the percentage of names above their 200-day average falling through 50% while the indices hold, which would mean the advance has narrowed further.

Method, and what this cannot see

Everything above traces to a stored observation with a date. The system ingests 71 series and 252 priced instruments from keyless sources, computes dials, signals, breadth, correlations, betas and flows deterministically, and runs language models only over precomputed numbers. No figure in this report was produced by a model from memory.

What it cannot see. No earnings estimates or revisions, which removes the single most robust cross-sectional equity signal from the toolkit. No valuation multiples, so every statement about a desk being cheap or expensive is a statement about price percentiles rather than about value. No positioning data outside CFTC futures. No management access, channel checks or proprietary data. Six desks have three or fewer news items, so those reads are price-only.

Known measurement problems, stated rather than hidden. Percentiles on trending price series restate the trend and are not evidence of cheapness or stretch. Monthly macro series carry 67-day-old prints at full weight. Correlations estimated on 55 observations across 23 assets are heavily noise-inflated and were debiased before use. The analogue sample is eight overlapping episodes from a five-year window. Signal hit rates do not yet exist because the audit loop started yesterday, so nothing here carries a track record.

One data correction made during this run. The Yahoo feed for the CSI 300 stopped updating on 17 July. It was replaced mid-analysis with a live A-share instrument, and a stale "China activity −2.7 sigma" signal that several agents had already cited was retired. Any reading of this report that includes a large negative China activity shock over the last five sessions is reading the artefact rather than the market: the current five-session move is +0.62 sigma.

The largest limitation is the one the desk analysts found. The dial board scores levels without momentum, weights stale drivers equally with live ones, and in six cases is a single-variable model wearing four variables. Until those are fixed, treat every stance on the board as a description of where drivers stand rather than a forecast of where they are going, and read the momentum rescoring alongside it.
